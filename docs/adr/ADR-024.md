# ADR-024: An unanswered data list holds the app to ASVS level 2

**Status:** accepted. Decided on 27 September 2026 at the owner's word ("let's fix the personal data starter file
issue"), built the same day (commit `b02efec`, merged through #265), and written down here on 4 October 2026, because
the first weekly review of the decision records found it had no record (BACKLOG, "Records owed", item 4).

**Governs:** `crates/sv-manifest/src/lib.rs`, `crates/sv-manifest/src/spec.rs`. A pull request that changes any of these
changes this record too, or says in its description why it does not (`docs/adr/README.md`).

## Context

OWASP ASVS has three levels, and the level an app is held to decides how many requirements apply. `sv` chooses it
from two answers in `securevibe.toml`: who the app is for (`[app] audience`), and what information it holds about
people (`[data] categories`). An app for customers or the public is held to level 2. So is one that holds anything on
the sensitive list (financial, payment-card, health, government-id, and children). An app for its owner alone or a
team, holding nothing sensitive, is held to level 1.

ADR-015 says that silence is not a "no": a question the owner has not answered leaves the requirements that turn on
it not assessed, never not applicable. It speaks of the yes-or-no facts that decide which requirements apply, and says
nothing of the level.

The starter file `sv init` wrote once held `categories = []`, which reads as "the app holds nothing about people". An
owner who never looked at that line got level 1, on a "no" they had never said.

## Decision

A data list nobody filled in counts as holding something sensitive, so the app is held to level 2. In the words
above `Manifest::target_level` (`crates/sv-manifest/src/lib.rs`): "level 1 is a claim that nothing sensitive is
held, and a list nobody filled in makes no claim."

- No `[data]` section, an empty one, or the line still commented out is unanswered: level 2.
- `categories = []` is an answer, "nothing about people", and with an audience of just the owner or a team gives
  level 1.
- The starter file now writes the line commented out (`# categories = ?`), with a comment saying what to put there.
- Only an app for its owner or a team can move: one for customers or the public is at level 2 by its audience,
  whatever the list says.

This carries ADR-015 into choosing the level, in the direction ADR-015 already takes: when unsure, more requirements
rather than fewer. It differs in one way. Under ADR-015 an unanswered question leaves its requirements not assessed;
here there is no requirement to leave unassessed, because the level decides which requirements apply at all, so the
unanswered list resolves to the stricter of the two answers.

## Alternatives considered

- **Level 1 when the list is unanswered.** What `sv` did before. Set aside for the reason in the Context: it turns
  silence into the answer that asks least of the app. Nothing else was written down as considered.
- **Not assessed.** Not workable for the level itself: every requirement's applicability depends on it, so "not
  assessed" would leave the whole report unassessed. Not recorded as considered at the time; said here as the reason
  the ADR-015 form could not be copied over unchanged.

## Consequences

- An owner who has not answered is held to more requirements than one who answers that the app holds nothing about
  people, which is the intent.
- The report says why. When the unanswered list is the only reason for level 2, `sv scope` prints a note and the report
  lists a gap, "What information the app holds about people", saying the level becomes 1 if nothing listed is
  sensitive (`Manifest::level_from_unanswered_data`). For an app for customers or the public the note is not shown,
  since the list is not what holds it at level 2.
- The owner lowers the level by answering, never by `sv` guessing: `sv` does not edit the manifest or change the level
  on the owner's behalf.
- `target_level` never returns 3. Level 3 requirements are counted only as "above this level".

## How it is held

`data_nobody_described_is_not_a_quiet_no` (unanswered, an empty `[data]`, and `[]`; the note only for an unanswered
list, and not for a public app), `sensitive_data_raises_the_target_level`, and
`the_starter_file_leaves_the_data_unanswered`, in `crates/sv-manifest/src/lib.rs`; and
`an_unanswered_data_list_is_held_to_level_2_and_says_why` in `crates/sv-cli/tests/data_unanswered.rs`, which runs
`sv report` both ways.

## Later, 6 October 2026: a data category `sv` does not know is not a quiet "no" either

The documentation review of 6 October 2026 (BACKLOG, item 7) found that only an exact match to a sensitive category
raised the level, so a misspelled `"helth"` or a capitalized `"Health"` quietly allowed level 1. The same rule as this
record's: a name `sv` cannot read is no answer that nothing sensitive is held. Names are now matched as the list spells
them, whatever their capitals and spaces (`" Health "` is `health`); a name not on the list (`DATA_CATEGORIES`, the
eleven the spec prints) holds the app to level 2, and the report and `sv check` say which name and why. The spec now
says so too.

## Later, 9 October 2026: the report says why the level, and on whose word

The gap analysis of 7 October 2026, finding 17, its first part. The two answers that set the app's level, its audience
and the data it holds, are in `stackvet.toml`, which the AI coding tool usually writes; nothing confirms them, and the
report said only "Held to ASVS level 1". A level 1 that rests on the tool's `audience = "just-me"` read as settled.

Under the level line, every report now says why (`Manifest::level_because`): the audience, the sensitive information
listed, the data list left unanswered, or names `sv` does not know, as this record's rule decides it. It says these
are answers the AI coding tool usually writes and nobody has confirmed. At level 1 it says how many more requirements
level 2 would bring, counting level 2's own and not level 3's (`report.json` carries both as `level_why`). The level
itself, and what applies at it, are unchanged: this says what the conclusion rests on, it does not change it. Sealing
the scope through `sv review`, and holding the answers to what the code shows, stay open in the backlog. Held by
`crates/sv-manifest/tests/level_because.rs` and `crates/sv-cli/tests/level_why.rs`.

## Later, 9 October 2026: at level 1, the report asks about what the code shows

The gap analysis of 7 October 2026, finding 17, its second part. The entry above says the answers that set the level
are unconfirmed; this one holds them to the app's own code. **When the app is held to level 1, the report looks in its
code for what would make it level 2, and asks the owner under the level line**: a sign-up route open to strangers
(`/signup`, `/register`, `/join`, and their like, as a quoted path) beside an audience of "only me" or "my team"; and
field names for health, financial, card, or identity information (`diagnosis`, `iban`, `card_number`, `ssn`, and their
like, as whole names, case and underscores aside) for a category the data list does not name. One question per kind,
at the first place it was seen, with its file and line; `report.json` carries them as `level_why.hints`.

- **A question, never a finding, and the level is unchanged.** A name in the code is a hint, not proof of what the
  app holds: `diagnosis` may be a car's. Raising the level on it would put a guess where this record puts the owner's
  answer. The question says that if the hint is right, changing the answer raises the level.
- **Only at level 1.** At level 2 there is nothing more the answers could add.
- **What is read:** code files by extension, never a test folder or a folder the manifest sets apart, since neither is
  the app as it runs. The names and routes are in `data/level-hints.json`, read through `sv_frameworks::data`
  (ADR-036), so a missed name is added without rebuilding `sv`; a list that cannot be read is said on standard error
  and asks nothing, which is not a pass.

Sealing the scope through `sv review` stays open in the backlog. Held by `crates/sv-check/tests/level_hints.rs` and
`crates/sv-cli/tests/level_hints_report.rs`; the design entry of the day lists the guards broken to test them.

## Later, 9 October 2026: the owner confirms the answers that set the level

The gap analysis of 7 October 2026, finding 17, its last part. The entries above say the two answers that set the
level are unconfirmed, and ask about what the code shows; this lets the owner confirm them. **`sv review`, after every
other entry, shows the audience and the data list and asks the owner to confirm them**; only `owner` is taken, since
only the owner knows who uses the app and what it holds. It writes `[scope-review]` with the answers as confirmed,
`by`, `on`, and a seal over all of them (ADR-043's seals, unchanged), and asks again whenever an answer no longer
matches what was confirmed. The categories are compared as the level reads them, case, spaces, and order aside; an
unanswered list is never the same as an empty one.

The level line then says whose word the level rests on: "answers in stackvet.toml that the owner confirmed through
`sv review` on 2026-10-09, signed with key …"; or that they "have changed since the owner confirmed them on …, so
they are unconfirmed again"; or that a confirmation "does not count here", and why, for an entry with no seal or one
that does not hold. With no entry, it says, as before, that nobody has confirmed them, and now names `sv review`.
`report.json` carries it as `level_why.confirmed`.

- **The level is unchanged in every case.** A confirmation says whose word it rests on; it does not raise or lower
  anything, and an unconfirmed answer is not treated as wrong.
- **Asked last and apart from the count.** Every app has these two answers, so the question would otherwise be
  the first entry of every review; asked after the others, "Recorded N of M" still counts the entries it did, and a
  review with nothing else waiting still asks it.

Held by `crates/sv-cli/src/review/scope_tests.rs` (9) and `crates/sv-cli/tests/scope_confirmed_report.rs` (9,
through the binary); the design entry of the day lists the guards broken to test them. With this, finding 17 is done.
